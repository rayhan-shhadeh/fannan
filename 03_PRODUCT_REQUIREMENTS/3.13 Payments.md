## 1. Overview

This document defines the payment experience for FANNAN. It covers payment methods, checkout authorization, refund and partial refund handling, failed payment recovery, payment statuses, invoicing, fraud prevention, settlement, and operational payment visibility.

The payment experience must be trustworthy, secure, transparent, and resilient. Buyers must understand what they are paying, when funds are captured or authorized, how payment failures are handled, and what status they can expect. Artists must be able to receive payouts according to approved settlement policy, while operations teams must be able to review and support payment exceptions without exposing sensitive card data or violating compliance requirements.

This document describes product behavior only. It does not define database fields, undocumented API endpoints, payment provider APIs, storage schemas, integration tokens, or internal implementation details. Exact technical contracts, provider implementation rules, reconciliation logic, and persisted payment records shall be defined in dedicated finance, billing, API, and compliance specifications before implementation.

## 4. Payment Providers

### 4.1 Purpose

Payment providers define the approved methods through which customers can complete purchases on FANNAN. The marketplace shall support a managed set of payment channels that are available by region, product category, and business policy.

### 4.2 Workflow

1. Customer selects a payment method during checkout.
2. System validates that the payment method is available for the order, region, and customer context.
3. System either initiates authorization or triggers the chosen cash or online payment path.
4. System returns a success, pending, or failure result with clear next steps.
5. Order status and payment status are updated in a consistent manner for the customer and support teams.

### 4.3 Business Rules

- FANNAN shall support only approved payment methods and providers that meet compliance and operational requirements.
- Payment options must be visible only when they are valid for the location, buyer profile, and order context.
- The available provider list must remain consistent with policy, region, and operational risk controls.
- Customers shall not be forced through a provider method that is unavailable or ineligible for their order.

### 4.4 Validation

- Payment provider eligibility must be checked against order value, delivery destination, buyer profile, and risk assessment.
- Unsupported or unavailable methods must be hidden or disabled with a clear reason in the UI when that reason is appropriate.
- Provider-level failures shall not cause silent order inconsistencies; the system must show the proper state and next actions.

### 4.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Select an approved payment method for a valid order. |
| Admin | Configure, review, and maintain approved provider availability by region or policy. |
| Artist | Observe settlement and payout outcomes associated with payment method activity as approved by operations. |

### 4.6 Edge Cases

- If a provider is temporarily unavailable, the system shall show the issue and allow the customer to retry or choose another valid method.
- If the order becomes invalid after provider selection, the system shall clear the stale payment state and revalidate the order.
- If the customer switches payment methods mid-flow, the system shall update the payment summary and revalidate against current order state.

### 4.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Only approved provider options are presented for eligible orders.
- The customer receives a clear success or failure outcome after payment attempt.
- A failed or unavailable provider does not leave the order in an ambiguous payment state.

## 5. Cash on Delivery

### 5.1 Purpose

Cash on Delivery allows eligible buyers to pay for an order when the goods are delivered, subject to policy, coverage, and risk rules. It must be used only where operationally allowed and clearly communicated to the customer.

### 5.2 Workflow

1. Customer selects Cash on Delivery during checkout if available.
2. System validates order eligibility, delivery destination, value thresholds, and risk policy.
3. Customer confirms order and receives clear payment instructions for delivery handling.
4. Order enters a pending payment or payable state until fulfillment and collection occur.
5. If the payment is not collected or is refused, the order shall be handled according to operational payment and fulfillment policy.

### 5.3 Business Rules

- Cash on Delivery shall be offered only in supported locations and for eligible order types.
- Customers shall be informed before submission of any value limits, pickup or delivery conditions, or collection requirements.
- Orders paid by cash on delivery must still be subject to address, product, shipping, and status validation.
- Refused or uncollected payments must trigger a documented follow-up process and clear customer communication.

### 5.4 Validation

- Cash on Delivery eligibility shall be checked at order creation and when the order is ready for collection or dispatch.
- High-risk, invalid, or unsupported destinations shall be blocked from this payment path.
- Customers must not receive a misleading confirmation that payment is complete when collection is still pending.

### 5.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Use Cash on Delivery only when available and eligible. |
| Admin | Enable, disable, or review COD availability and exceptions by region or policy. |
| Artist | Monitor COD order status and payout timing according to policy. |

### 5.6 Edge Cases

- If the buyer is not available to pay on delivery, the order must enter a managed failed-collection path.
- If a COD order is canceled before delivery, the system shall prevent any assumption of collected payment.
- If the delivery destination becomes invalid after order creation, the fulfillment team shall follow the operational exception process.

### 5.7 Acceptance Criteria

- COD is only available when business and regional rules permit it.
- The customer understands that payment is due upon delivery and that non-payment may affect delivery or order status.
- COD orders are clearly distinguishable from prepaid orders in state handling and support workflows.

## 6. Online Payments

### 6.1 Purpose

Online payments provide the primary digital payment experience for customer orders. They allow fast, secure payment authorization and confirmation for eligible purchases on FANNAN.

### 6.2 Workflow

1. Customer selects an online payment method during checkout.
2. System validates the order, destination, and pricing.
3. Customer authorizes the transaction through the approved provider flow.
4. System receives authorization or approval result and updates order and payment states.
5. If the payment succeeds, the order proceeds to confirmation and fulfillment; if it fails, the customer is returned to a safe retry path.

### 6.3 Business Rules

- Online payment must be available only for approved methods and supported regions.
- The system shall inform the customer before charging if pre-authorization or capture timing applies.
- A payment shall not be considered complete until the provider result has been validated against the order state.
- Duplicate payment attempts must be prevented through valid transaction state checks and retry safeguards.

### 6.4 Validation

- Payment result must match the expected order and amount before the order is marked as paid.
- Payment attempt success must be revalidated against cart state, coupon state, shipping state, and order totals.
- Payment errors must be communicated clearly without exposing underlying provider errors unless appropriate and authorized.

### 6.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Initiate online payment for eligible orders. |
| Admin | Review online payment failures, dispute conditions, and provider outages in an approved support workflow. |
| Artist | See payment status and payout outcomes, not sensitive card data. |

### 6.6 Edge Cases

- If the customer is redirected away during provider authorization, the system shall restore the order state and guide the customer to recovery.
- If the provider returns a timeout, the system shall prevent duplicate capture while offering retry or status review.
- If the provider approves a payment but the order is later invalidated, the system must handle the payment status in a controlled exception path.

### 6.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A valid online payment can be initiated and completed for eligible orders.
- Payment success is not confirmed before validation against the current order state.
- Failed or interrupted payments lead to clear and recoverable next steps.

## 7. Refunds

### 7.1 Purpose

Refunds provide a structured way to return funds to the customer when an order is canceled, not fulfilled, rejected, or otherwise eligible for repayment under policy. Refunds must be transparent and traceable.

### 7.2 Workflow

1. A refund case is initiated by customer support, self-service refund criteria, or order lifecycle rules.
2. System validates the eligibility of the refund against order, payment, and business policy.
3. System calculates the refund amount and confirms the applicable method and timing.
4. Refund is processed or queued according to approved operational rules.
5. Customer and required internal teams receive the updated status and confirmation details.

### 7.3 Business Rules

- Refunds shall only be issued when payment has been received or a valid eligibility condition exists.
- Refund eligibility and amount must be clearly communicated to the customer and support teams.
- Refund timing shall be consistent with the payment method and operational policy.
- Refund actions must be auditable and reversible only under appropriate exception workflow.

### 7.4 Validation

- Refund requests shall validate the original order, invoice, payment record, and any cancellation or dispute conditions.
- Refund amounts must not exceed valid payment totals or approved partial amounts.
- The system must prevent duplicate refund processing for the same order or payment event.

### 7.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Request a refund when refund conditions are met and the workflow is available. |
| Admin | Approve or process refunds in the approved support or operations path. |
| Artist | Receive refund status impacts relevant to order settlement or support resolution as appropriate. |

### 7.6 Edge Cases

- If a refund request conflicts with an already processed payment dispute, the system shall route to the exception process.
- If a customer expects a refund but the payment method is no longer available, the system shall follow policy for alternate return paths or support review.
- If a refund becomes partial after shipping or fulfillment adjustments, the system shall calculate the correct amount without ambiguity.

### 7.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A valid refund request results in clear customer communication and a consistent payment state.
- Refunds cannot exceed the eligible payment amount or approved policy.
- Refund outcomes are visible in order and payment history.

## 8. Partial Refunds

### 8.1 Purpose

Partial refunds support situations where only a portion of an order, product line, shipping adjustment, or fee is returned. This avoids over-refunding while respecting customer expectations and operational fairness.

### 8.2 Workflow

1. System identifies the eligible refund component or adjustment.
2. Support or policy engine calculates the partial amount based on order value, fulfillment status, and agreed settlement terms.
3. Customer is informed of the amount and reason.
4. Refund is processed for the approved amount and the remaining balance remains as originally paid or due.
5. Order and invoice records reflect the partial refund accurately.

### 8.3 Business Rules

- Partial refunds shall be allowed only where policy permits a proportional or item-level reduction.
- The system shall clearly differentiate between full and partial refunds in customer-facing status and admin review.
- A partial refund shall not overwrite the original payment status in a way that hides the remaining balance or obligation.

### 8.4 Validation

- Partial refund calculations must be based on valid, approved adjustment logic and not on manual approximations.
- The sum of refund amounts must not exceed the original payment amount without a documented exception.
- Refund results must remain consistent across payment logs, order history, and invoice records.

### 8.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Receive partial refunds when the policy allows and the case is approved. |
| Admin | Process or approve partial refund adjustments in operational workflows. |
| Artist | See the effect of partial refunds on settlement and order resolution where relevant. |

### 8.6 Edge Cases

- If a partial refund is requested after an earlier whole-order refund, the system shall block the duplicate or conflicting adjustment.
- If multiple items or fees are involved, the system shall preserve the distinction between product adjustments, shipping adjustments, and service-related refunds.
- If the partial refund eligibility changes after fulfillment, the system shall return to a valid exception path instead of silently recalculating.

### 8.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A valid partial refund is executed for the approved amount only.
- The customer sees a clear explanation of the partial refund and its basis.
- Order and settlement history remain internally consistent.

## 9. Failed Payments

### 9.1 Purpose

Failed payments cover any payment attempt that cannot be completed successfully due to insufficient funds, provider rejection, risk block, expired authorization, or technical failure. Customer recovery and operational review must be clear and safe.

### 9.2 Workflow

1. Payment attempt fails or is rejected.
2. System captures the failure reason as appropriate, without exposing sensitive payment details unnecessarily.
3. Customer is guided to retry, choose another method, or resolve the issue.
4. Order remains protected from silent state changes or unauthorized completion.
5. Support or admin workflows can review failed attempts if needed.

### 9.3 Business Rules

- Failed payment attempts shall never lead to a completed order without a successful authorization result.
- The system shall provide a clear, actionable failure message and recovery options.
- Payment failures must not cause data loss or silently clear the customer’s cart or order intent.
- Repeated failed attempts must be tracked for risk and support review without exposing sensitive details.

### 9.4 Validation

- Payment failure state must be revalidated against the current order and any user action before allowing a retry.
- The system shall block duplicate or stale retries when a payment is already in progress or completed.
- Failed payment records shall remain visible for review and support without exposing card or provider data.

### 9.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Retry a failed payment or choose another supported method. |
| Admin | Review failed payment context, fraud indicators, and order support outcomes. |
| Artist | Review impacted order state without direct access to payment details. |

### 9.6 Edge Cases

- If the customer attempts a retry after a valid payment was already captured, the system shall prevent duplicate charging.
- If the provider returns a temporary network error, the system shall keep the order in a safe pending or retry state.
- If a payment is blocked due to policy or risk, the customer shall receive an actionable explanation and not a generic failure.

### 9.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Failed payments do not result in an order being marked paid or shipped.
- Customers can retry or choose alternative valid payment methods.
- Failed payment history remains visible for support and operational review.

## 10. Payment Statuses

### 10.1 Purpose

Payment statuses provide a consistent way to communicate the state of each payment event to customers, artists, and operations teams. The status model must be understandable and consistent across the lifecycle from initiation to settlement.

### 10.2 Workflow

1. Payment is created or initialized from the order.
2. System updates the payment status as it moves through authorization, verification, completion, refund, or failure states.
3. Customer-facing and operational views show the appropriate status without exposing sensitive details.
4. Status changes are preserved for order, invoice, and support workflows.

### 10.3 Business Rules

- Payment status shall reflect the current state of the transaction, not merely the customer’s intent.
- Status definitions must remain consistent across app, admin operations, and support workflows.
- A payment status shall never imply settlement or refund completion unless the corresponding operational state is valid.
- Customers shall be able to distinguish pending, successful, failed, refunded, or adjusted payment states.

### 10.4 Validation

- State transitions must follow documented lifecycle rules without skipping required checkpoints.
- Customer and admin views shall show the same high-level status semantics.
- Completed or failed states shall be accompanied by the relevant action or next-step information where necessary.

### 10.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View order payment status and relevant order updates. |
| Artist | View payment and settlement status related to their sales and payouts. |
| Admin | Review full payment lifecycle status and support-related actions. |

### 10.6 Edge Cases

- If the provider returns a status that cannot be mapped cleanly, the transaction must be routed to operational review instead of assumed success.
- If there is a mismatch between order status and payment status, the system must surface a clear exception state.
- If partial payments or split settlements occur, the status model must reflect that clearly without ambiguity.

### 10.7 Acceptance Criteria

- The payment lifecycle is visible and understandable across customer-facing and admin workflows.
- Status transitions are governed by the defined rules and never silently skip major states.
- Critical mismatches between order and payment state are surfaced for review.

## 11. Invoices

### 11.1 Purpose

Invoices provide a formal record of payment, order value, fees, adjustments, and fulfillment context. They support customer transparency, tax or compliance review, and settlement visibility for the marketplace.

### 11.2 Workflow

1. Order is confirmed or paid.
2. System generates or updates an invoice record associated with the order and payment state.
3. Customer can view or download the invoice in the applicable lifecycle state.
4. Invoice records reflect adjustments, refunds, or partial payment events as allowed by policy.
5. Admin and support teams can reference invoice records during disputes or operational review.

### 11.3 Business Rules

- Invoices shall represent the final approved order value and any associated payment, fee, or adjustment records relevant to the transaction.
- Customers must be able to access a copy of their invoice when the order or payment status allows it.
- Invoices must remain consistent with order state and refund or partial refund activity.
- Invoices shall not expose sensitive payment data beyond the approved operational requirements.

### 11.4 Validation

- Invoice values must match the current order summary and any approved payment adjustments.
- Refund or correction events must be reflected in the invoice status without invalidating historic records.
- A customer must not receive a misleading invoice for a non-finalized order state.

### 11.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View and download valid invoices for their order history. |
| Artist | Access invoices applicable to their sales activity when permitted by policy. |
| Admin | Review, manage, and correct invoice support cases when necessary. |

### 11.6 Edge Cases

- If a payment is refunded or partially refunded, the invoice must reflect the final settled value appropriately.
- If an order is canceled before payment, the system shall avoid issuing or publishing a misleading invoice.
- If a payment method or provider is changed after initial confirmation, the invoice shall remain aligned with the accepted transaction state.

### 11.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Customers can access their order invoices in a clear and legally usable format when permitted.
- Invoices remain synchronized with order and payment adjustments.
- Invoice availability is consistent across customer and support workflows.

## 12. Fraud Prevention

### 12.1 Purpose

Fraud prevention protects the marketplace from payment abuse, transaction abuse, fake orders, high-risk behavior, and suspicious patterns while preserving a smooth buyer experience for legitimate customers.

### 12.2 Workflow

1. Order and payment attempt are evaluated against approved risk and business checks.
2. System flags suspicious patterns or policy mismatches before or during payment.
3. Customer is informed of the issue or the transaction is routed to approval or review.
4. Legitimate orders continue when risk checks pass; suspicious transactions are held or escalated.
5. Admin or support team reviews flagged cases according to operational policy.

### 12.3 Business Rules

- Fraud prevention shall be applied without creating unnecessary friction for legitimate buyers.
- Risk responses must be clear, consistent, and safe; blocked transactions must not silently fail without guidance.
- High-risk or suspicious order activity must be traceable and reviewable by the appropriate teams.
- Fraud controls shall never allow sensitive payment data to be exposed in normal customer flows.

### 12.4 Validation

- Payment and order attempts must meet policy conditions before completion.
- Repeated risk triggers or mismatches must be used to inform support and risk decisions without revealing internal rule details beyond what is necessary.
- Risk exceptions must include a documented review and recovery path.

### 12.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Continue a valid order flow when checks pass; receive clear update when blocked for risk reasons. |
| Admin | Review and resolve fraud-related payment exceptions. |
| Artist | Be notified of blocked or risky payment situations only as required for order transparency and internal support. |

### 12.6 Edge Cases

- If a user is flagged for suspicious behavior despite a valid order, the system shall not disclose internal fraud logic in a way that undermines trust.
- If a legitimate customer is temporarily blocked due to a policy mismatch, the system shall provide a path to resolve or escalate the case.
- If a provider returns a fraud decline, the order must remain recoverable without causing duplicate processing.

### 12.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Fraud checks can prevent abusive transactions without unnecessary disruption to valid buyers.
- Customers receive clear, actionable guidance when a transaction is blocked for risk reasons.
- Admins can review and manage suspicious payment outcomes through the approved workflow.

## 13. PCI Considerations

### 13.1 Purpose

PCI considerations define the product behavior required to protect payment data and minimize risk. The system must support secure payment handling consistent with compliance expectations without exposing card data in user-facing or internal workflows where it should not be kept.

### 13.2 Workflow

1. Customer enters payment information in an approved secure payment experience.
2. System routes payment data to the authorized provider or secure payment channel.
3. The user is kept away from sensitive raw card data where applicable.
4. Support and admin ops access payment status and logs without direct access to card data except under approved exception procedures.

### 13.3 Business Rules

- Payment card data shall be handled only in approved secure paths and not stored in places that are not designed for that purpose.
- The product shall support secure authorizations and customer confidence without exposing raw card details in app pages, logs, or unapproved support channels.
- Sensitive payment data must be masked or excluded from customer-visible histories and admin views where appropriate.
- The platform must support the operational processes required for compliance review and incident handling.

### 13.4 Validation

- High-risk exposure points must be blocked or reviewed before user-facing release.
- Payment flows must remain compliant with security and privacy expectations across web, mobile, and support contexts.
- Any exception handling for sensitive data must be explicitly controlled and traceable.

### 13.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Provide payment details only in secure, approved flows. |
| Admin | Review payment status and operational history without unnecessary access to sensitive payment details. |
| Artist | Access only the order and settlement data relevant to their role. |

### 13.6 Edge Cases

- If a payment method requires a secure token or redirect, the system shall maintain a clear and safe customer experience without exposing hidden implementation details.
- If sensitive data is accidentally exposed to an unauthorized context, the operational incident path must be triggered as policy requires.
- If support needs to investigate a payment issue, the system shall route to approved handling rather than allowing uncontrolled data access.

### 13.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Sensitive card data is not exposed through standard user interfaces or general support workflows.
- Payment handling follows approved secure processing behavior and compliance constraints.
- Operational teams can support payment issues without creating unnecessary exposure to payment data.

## 14. Payment Logs

### 14.1 Purpose

Payment logs support investigation, auditability, support, and operational monitoring. They provide a trustworthy record of key payment events without exposing sensitive card data or internal implementation details.

### 14.2 Workflow

1. A payment event occurs, including authorization, completion, rejection, refund, or settlement state transitions.
2. System records the event in the approved operational log history.
3. Admins and support teams can review the payment event and its resolution path.
4. The log supports troubleshooting and operational reporting without disclosing sensitive payment payloads.

### 14.3 Business Rules

- Payment logs shall reflect status transitions and operationally relevant payment events in a traceable manner.
- Logs must be consistent with the payment lifecycle and not contradict the observed order state.
- Logs shall be retained and accessible only to authorized operational roles.
- Sensitive payment details must be excluded from standard logs, summaries, and customer-visible views.

### 14.4 Validation

- Payment logs must align with valid order and payment states.
- Admin and support levels must be able to trace key events without exposing restricted data.
- Log integrity must be maintained across retries, failures, reversals, and settlement events.

### 14.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View payment status and order-related history, but not internal payment logs containing sensitive or operational details. |
| Admin | View and investigate payment logs according to job role and policy. |
| Artist | Access only the payment-related summaries necessary for settlement and support. |

### 14.6 Edge Cases

- If a payment event results in an exception or mismatch, the system shall include a clear status and escalation path in the log.
- If the provider is unreachable during payment processing, the log must show the event without claiming a final payment state.
- If the same order is retried or recovered, the log must preserve sequence and chronology for support review.

### 14.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Payment activity is traceable through an approved operational log.
- Payment logs are not used to expose payment data beyond approved access boundaries.
- Support and admin teams can investigate failed, refunded, or disputed transactions efficiently.

## 15. Settlement

### 15.1 Purpose

Settlement defines how funds are moved from the marketplace to the relevant parties after a successful payment, including artist payouts and marketplace obligations. It must be clear, predictable, and aligned with policy.

### 15.2 Workflow

1. Payment is completed and matched to the applicable order.
2. System identifies eligible payout amounts and any fees, holds, or deductions defined by policy.
3. Settlement is queued or initiated in the designated operational flow.
4. Artists or other stakeholders receive payout status updates and available payout details.
5. Admins review exceptions and support settlement issues when necessary.

### 15.3 Business Rules

- Settlement must align with the approved payment outcome and the applicable fee, commission, or payout policy.
- Artists must receive transparent information about payout timing, status, and any conditions affecting settlement.
- Pending, successful, partial, or failed settlement states shall be distinguishable in the relevant views.
- The platform must preserve a clear audit trail for settlement activity and exceptions.

### 15.4 Validation

- Settlement eligibility must be revalidated against order state, payment status, refund status, and approved policy.
- Settlements must not be released when there are unresolved blocked or disputed conditions.
- Customer refunds, chargebacks, or dispute adjustments must be reflected in settlement calculations.

### 15.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Artist | View settlement status and relevant payout information for their sales. |
| Customer | View payment and refund status, not internal settlement details for other parties. |
| Admin | Review settlements, exceptions, and payout workflows across the marketplace. |

### 15.6 Edge Cases

- If a refund is issued after settlement has begun, the system must handle the payout adjustment under policy and operational review.
- If a settlement is delayed due to risk or compliance review, the status must be escalated clearly.
- If a payout amount changes because of a corrected order or invoice, the system must keep the latest valid value in the settlement record.

### 15.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A successful payment leads to a valid settlement process according to policy.
- Refund and dispute activities are reflected in settlement decisions and reporting.
- Artists and admins can understand the settlement status and any exception path.

## 16. Future Wallet

### 16.1 Purpose

The Future Wallet capability allows customers and artists to hold and manage funds for future purchases, payouts, or marketplace credits. This feature is a future product extension and must be introduced under clearly defined policy and compliance rules.

### 16.2 Workflow

1. User qualifies for wallet capability under the future marketplace policy.
2. System loads wallet balance and relevant wallet status information.
3. User may use wallet funds for eligible purchases or receive approved payouts, credits, or refunds.
4. Wallet balance and transaction history remain consistent with payment, refund, and settlement events.
5. Wallet-related actions remain subject to platform policy, risk controls, and customer support review.

### 16.3 Business Rules

- Wallet functionality must be enabled only where policy, compliance, and operational support are ready.
- Wallet balances shall remain consistent with payment, refund, and settlement activity.
- Wallet statements and histories must distinguish between deposited funds, spent funds, refunds, and operational adjustments.
- Wallet access must be protected by the same account and identity safeguards as other payment-related features.

### 16.4 Validation

- Wallet activity must always reconcile to valid payment and settlement events.
- Risk or compliance checks must apply to wallet use before funds are spent or released.
- Wallet balances must never be shown as available when they are restricted, pending, or unavailable under policy.

### 16.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View wallet status and eligible wallet transactions when the feature is activated. |
| Artist | View wallet-related payout or balance visibility when permitted by future policy. |
| Admin | Review wallet activity, exceptions, and restrictions in the approved operational workflow. |

### 16.6 Edge Cases

- If wallet funds are withheld due to a dispute or refund adjustment, the system shall maintain a clear status rather than silently omitting the balance.
- If wallet balances are adjusted because of a failed settlement or correction, the balance must be recalculated consistently.
- If the wallet feature is unavailable in a region or for a customer segment, the system must hide or disable the option without creating misleading states.

### 16.7 Acceptance Criteria

- The wallet feature is only enabled in approved scenarios and with clear policy controls.
- Wallet balances match underlying payment and settlement events.
- Support and operational teams can review wallet exceptions without exposing sensitive payment data.

## 17. Cross-Cutting Requirements

### 17.1 Customer Communication

- All payment states must be communicated with plain language, clear timing expectations, and actionable next steps.
- Customers must not be left uncertain whether payment was completed, pending, failed, or refunded.
- Support messages related to payment should explain how to recover or escalate without exposing sensitive technical details.

### 17.2 Admin and Support Operations

- Admins must have access to the payment lifecycle and relevant support context required for dispute resolution, failed payment handling, and settlement review.
- Payment support workflows must preserve traceability and avoid exposing unnecessary data to the wrong role.

### 17.3 Security and Compliance

- Payment handling must align with approved security controls and operational compliance requirements.
- Any requirements for tokenization, provider routing, exception handling, or restricted access must be treated as policy requirements and not as ad hoc implementation assumptions.

### 17.4 Accessibility and Localization

- Payment and refund workflows must be usable through keyboard, screen-reader, and responsive layouts.
- The product must support Arabic and English customer communication, with correct RTL/LTR token and status presentation.
- Payment error and success messages must be understandable to users with diverse contexts and accessibility needs.

## 18. Acceptance Summary

- Customers can complete valid orders using supported payment methods with clear status feedback.
- Refunds, partial refunds, failed payments, and order-payment mismatches are handled through safe, reviewable paths.
- Payment providers, settlement, fraud protection, and operational review operate within policy and compliance boundaries.
- Invoices, logs, and payment states remain transparent and traceable without exposing sensitive payment data.
- Future wallet functionality is defined as a clearly scoped extension and is not treated as a current default capability.
