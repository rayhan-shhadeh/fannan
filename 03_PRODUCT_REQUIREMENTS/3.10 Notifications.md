## 1. Overview

This document defines the notification system for FANNAN. It covers the communication channels, triggers, trust and safety requirements, preference controls, quiet hours, localization, and accessibility expectations for buyer, artist, and staff notifications.

FANNAN notifications must provide timely, relevant, and understandable updates without becoming disruptive or intrusive. The system shall support a clear, role-aware notification experience across web and mobile while preserving privacy and respecting user consent. A notification is a communication action that may appear in email, push, in-app center, or a future SMS channel; it must be governed by documented business rules, delivery policy, and user-preference controls.

This document describes product behavior only. It does not define database tables, undocumented API endpoints, transport provider contracts, message payload formats, or delivery internals. Exact request and response shapes, persisted fields, event taxonomy, message templates, retention rules, provider integrations, and queue behavior shall be documented in the relevant API, data model, notification, and infrastructure specifications before implementation.

## 4. Email

### 4.1 Purpose

Email notifications provide a durable, accessible communication channel for account activation, important account updates, purchase or sales status, support updates, and other high-value events that benefit from a message history or that may be opened later.

### 4.2 Workflow

1. A documented business event is generated for a user or staff recipient.
2. The system checks whether the user has enabled email notifications for the relevant category and whether the address is valid and active.
3. The system selects an approved template and priority level.
4. The system sends the email through the documented email channel with the correct localization, sender identity, and business-safe content.
5. The system records delivery status and outcome so support and operations can review failures or retries.

### 4.3 Business Rules

- Email notifications shall follow user preference settings and consent status.
- Email shall be used for important, traceable, or time-sensitive communication when a user is expected to engage asynchronously.
- Email content must not expose private or sensitive information beyond what the user and policy allow.
- Account security and payment-related events shall use clear subject lines and minimal sensitive content.
- Email notifications shall never be used to send content that is blocked by the user’s preferences or privacy rules.

### 4.4 Validation

- Recipient email address validity, preference state, and current status shall be validated before sending.
- Localization, subject formatting, and fallback text must be validated for supported languages.
- Security-sensitive events shall be reviewed against the documented safe-content policy before delivery.

### 4.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Receive email notifications for subscribed categories and account-relevant events. |
| Artist | Receive email notifications for marketplace and account events where enabled. |
| Admin | Configure email templates, campaign or operational settings, and monitor status. |

### 4.6 Edge Cases

- If the email address is invalid or temporarily unavailable, the system shall not show a false success state.
- If a user is in quiet hours or has disabled a category, the system shall suppress or defer the email as permitted by policy.
- If the email is undeliverable, the system shall mark it as failed and apply retry logic according to the documented strategy.

### 4.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A user receives email for approved events if the channel is enabled and permitted.
- Account-critical and transaction-related communications are delivered with clear and safe subject content.
- Failed or blocked deliveries are clearly tracked without implying success.

## 5. Push

### 5.1 Purpose

Push notifications provide timely, user-visible alerts for real-time or near-real-time events, such as order updates, replies from artists or customers, payment change notifications, or marketplace actions that warrant immediate attention.

### 5.2 Workflow

1. A trigger event is created for a user or group of users.
2. The system checks the user’s push preference, device registration status, and delivery policy.
3. The system selects a priority, template, and fallback and prepares the push payload.
4. The push service delivers the notification to the user device when the user is eligible and the channel is available.
5. The system records result, suppression, or failure state for operational monitoring.

### 5.3 Business Rules

- Push notifications shall respect user permission, device registration, and quiet-hours settings.
- Push shall be reserved for actionable and time-sensitive updates rather than routine informational noise.
- The user must be able to disable or limit push notifications without affecting core account functionality.
- Push content must remain concise, clear, and actionable while avoiding sensitive data exposure.

### 5.4 Validation

- Device eligibility and registration state shall be checked before payload creation.
- Channel-specific payload limits and localized text shall be validated before dispatch.
- Quiet hours and priority rules shall be enforced before sending a push notification.

### 5.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Receive push for the categories and events they enabled. |
| Artist | Receive push for the relevant marketplace and account events they enabled. |
| Admin | Configure push templates and monitor delivery outcomes. |

### 5.6 Edge Cases

- If the device is offline or unregistered, the push shall be deferred or abandoned according to policy and not counted as a successful delivery.
- If a user disables push after a trigger is created, the system shall suppress the delivery if the event is still pending.
- If a push fails due to provider limits or delivery error, the system shall trigger the next recovery step according to the retry strategy.

### 5.7 Acceptance Criteria

- A user receives push notifications only for enabled, relevant, and eligible events.
- Critical updates are delivered in a timely manner without breaking quiet-hours or preference rules.
- Failed push delivery does not misreport success to the user or operation teams.

## 6. In-App

### 6.1 Purpose

In-app notifications keep users informed of updates while inside the app or on the web experience. They support session awareness and allow users to review notification history from the notification center without constant interruption.

### 6.2 Workflow

1. A system event occurs that qualifies for in-app delivery.
2. The system checks whether the user has enabled the category and whether the notification is eligible for display within the current session or feed.
3. The system creates the in-app update, associates it with the correct notification center entry, and may surface a toast or banner depending on priority.
4. The user can open the notification center to view, act on, or dismiss the message.

### 6.3 Business Rules

- In-app notifications shall be visible in the notification center and, when relevant, may also appear as a banner or badge in the UI.
- Users must be able to view and manage in-app notifications from a single consistent notification center.
- Important or time-sensitive items shall not disappear silently when a user is active in the application.
- In-app notifications shall not expose information beyond the approved context and role scope.

### 6.4 Validation

- Notification category, read state, and user visibility rules shall be validated before display.
- Duplicate in-app entries for the same event shall be prevented when the system applies the same notification logic repeatedly.
- Notification center item ordering shall reflect the documented source of truth for timestamp and priority.

### 6.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View and manage notifications relevant to their account and marketplace activity. |
| Artist | View and manage notifications relevant to their sales, portfolio, messages, and account activity. |
| Admin | View staff notifications and operational alerts where role-appropriate. |

### 6.6 Edge Cases

- If the user has already read a notification, the system shall not reinsert it as a new item without an actual new event.
- If the app is offline, in-app notifications may be queued for later delivery if the app supports that workflow; otherwise they shall remain unsent without false success states.
- If a notification becomes obsolete after a state change, the system shall update or clear it according to policy.

### 6.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Users can access all in-app notifications in a single notification center or equivalent centralized view.
- Event-driven notifications appear in the correct app context and are not duplicated unintentionally.
- Users can distinguish read, unread, urgent, and dismissed items without ambiguity.

## 7. SMS (Future)

### 7.1 Purpose

SMS may be introduced in a future phase for customer-critical, time-sensitive events where a brief, direct message is needed and the user has chosen the channel. SMS is explicitly future-facing and shall be added only with approved provider contracts and policy review.

### 7.2 Workflow

1. The system determines that a message qualifies for a future SMS channel.
2. User consent, regional limitations, and phone verification requirements are checked.
3. The system prepares the approved short-form message and sends it through the licensed SMS provider.
4. The system records delivery status and escalates failures according to the retry policy.

### 7.3 Business Rules

- SMS shall be opt-in and available only where the region, product, and policy permit it.
- SMS shall not be used for sensitive operational or account recovery communication unless specifically approved by security policy.
- SMS content shall be concise, clear, and limited to essential information.
- SMS must adhere to strict user consent and compliance requirements before activation.

### 7.4 Validation

- Phone number validity, consent status, and region support shall be validated before dispatch.
- Message length and compatibility with the approved provider shall be checked.
- Messaging flows shall be restricted to approved use cases only.

### 7.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Opt-in to SMS only when the platform has approved the channel and region. |
| Artist | Opt-in to SMS only when approved by platform policy. |
| Admin | Configure future SMS channel policy and monitor operational status only after launch approval. |

### 7.6 Edge Cases

- If a phone number changes or is unverified, the system shall suppress SMS and prompt the user to update preferences.
- If SMS is not supported in a region or for the account type, the system shall show the appropriate “not available” state.
- If SMS delivery is throttled or rejected by the provider, the system shall apply the documented fallback or retry strategy.

### 7.7 Acceptance Criteria

- SMS remains unavailable unless explicitly enabled and documented under the approved product and compliance policy.
- Users receive only approved, consented SMS messages in supported regions.
- Failed SMS delivery does not create a false indication of success.

## 8. Triggers

### 8.1 Purpose

Triggers define the system events that can generate notifications. They control when a notification is created, which channel should be used, and whether the notification is suppressed or escalated by policy.

### 8.2 Workflow

1. A business or operational event occurs in the marketplace or account lifecycle.
2. The system identifies the relevant notification rules and recipients.
3. The system evaluates channel availability, urgency, user preferences, and quiet hours.
4. The system creates the notification or defers it according to policy.
5. The system records the event and respects the delivery flow for the chosen channel.

### 8.3 Business Rules

- Triggers must be derived from documented platform events and approved notification metadata.
- Trigger rules shall never create notifications for unauthorized recipients or hidden data states.
- Duplicate event handling must be prevented when the same underlying event is processed more than once.
- System-critical events must be prioritized correctly without overriding a user’s explicit suppression rules.

### 8.4 Validation

- Event source and user/entity relationship shall be validated before notification creation.
- Trigger conditions must be checked against the user’s preferences and role scope.
- Filter logic must prevent redundant or conflicting notifications for the same event.

### 8.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Configure notification preferences for the categories they are eligible to receive. |
| Artist | Configure notification preferences for their marketplace and account activity. |
| Admin | Define and monitor trigger logic and operational policy for the notification system. |

### 8.6 Edge Cases

- If the event is repeated or retried, the system shall deduplicate the notification where the same actionable message is already outstanding.
- If a user changes preferences after a trigger is created, the system shall honor the current policy and suppress or delay the notification appropriately.
- If a trigger references a deleted or inaccessible record, the system shall discard or anonymize the notification if policy requires.

### 8.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Each approved event creates no more than the intended number of relevant notifications for the correct users.
- Alerts are suppressed or deferred when policy or user preferences require it.
- Duplicate or stale trigger events do not create misleading or repeated user experiences.

## 9. Templates

### 9.1 Purpose

Templates define the reusable notification copy and layout used for different channels and scenarios. They ensure consistent messaging, authorized content, and correct localization.

### 9.2 Workflow

1. Admin or support staff identifies a new or changed notification scenario.
2. The system reviews the approved notification policy and content requirements.
3. A template is created or updated with the correct text, variables, localization keys, and channel-specific constraints.
4. The template is tested or reviewed for language quality, tone, and accessibility requirements before release.

### 9.3 Business Rules

- Templates shall be approved, reusable, and channel-specific when necessary.
- Template text shall remain truthful, non-deceptive, and aligned with the business event it describes.
- Templates shall support Arabic and English and must provide approved fallback content when a locale is unavailable.
- Sensitive or regulated information shall remain outside generic notification copy and must use safe, minimal phrasing.

### 9.4 Validation

- Variables and placeholders used in templates must be validated against the event payload and supported locale.
- Template content must undergo review for readability, tone, length, and accessibility before release.
- Channel-specific constraints such as subject lines, push text length, and SMS formatting are validated separately.

### 9.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Admin | Full template creation, approval, and maintenance control. |
| Support Agent | Limited template review or operational content support when explicitly allowed. |

### 9.6 Edge Cases

- If a template is missing a translation for an active locale, the system shall use the approved fallback or show the message in the default supported language.
- If a placeholder value is missing, the system shall produce a safe fallback rather than a broken or misleading message.
- If a template is retired or deprecated, the system shall stop using it without interrupting active scheduled jobs unexpectedly.

### 9.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Approved templates are used consistently across all valid event types and channels.
- Templates respect channel limits and localization requirements.
- Unsupported or incomplete templates are not sent to users.

## 10. Priority Levels

### 10.1 Purpose

Priority levels determine how important a notification is to the recipient and how it should be surfaced across channels. They ensure that urgent operational or account events are attention-worthy without making all notifications equally disruptive.

### 10.2 Workflow

1. A system event qualifies for notification generation.
2. The system associates the event with the correct priority level according to the business logic.
3. The system selects channel behavior, delivery approach, and user-facing presentation based on the priority and user settings.
4. The user receives the message according to the channel and quiet-hours policy.

### 10.3 Business Rules

- Notification priority shall be tied to business urgency, impact, and actionability.
- High-priority alerts shall be escalated appropriately without overriding explicit user preference controls that disable the category.
- Low-priority or informational items shall be available in the notification center without aggressive interruption.
- Staff and customer notifications shall not be mixed without respect to role, policy, and channel scope.

### 10.4 Validation

- Priority classification must be validated against the event type and documented rules.
- Quiet hours and preference states shall be checked for all priority levels before dispatch.
- Priority configuration shall not produce endless escalation loops or repeated urgent prompts for the same event.

### 10.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View and manage priority-related notification preferences in their account settings. |
| Artist | Manage their own priority preferences for relevant marketplace events. |
| Admin | Define and monitor default priority policy for system notifications. |

### 10.6 Edge Cases

- If the user has disabled a category, the system shall not send even a high-priority notification for that category.
- If two urgent events occur at the same time, the system shall deduplicate or combine them according to policy rather than sending noisy duplicates.
- If a user has muted notifications for a period, the system shall suppress non-critical urgent notifications only where policy allows it.

### 10.7 Acceptance Criteria

- High-priority events are surfaced more prominently than routine updates.
- Routine notifications remain visible but not disruptive.
- Priority handling remains consistent across email, push, and in-app notifications.

## 11. Scheduling

### 11.1 Purpose

Scheduling controls when notifications are sent and when follow-ups or reminders should be queued. This supports deployment timing, quiet hours, and customer communication windows without causing unnecessary disruption.

### 11.2 Workflow

1. A trigger has passed the delivery eligibility check.
2. The system checks the user’s notification schedule, quiet hours, and documented business timing rules.
3. The system schedules the notification for the right time or defers it until the next allowed window.
4. The system respects updates to timing or cancellation when the user or policy changes.

### 11.3 Business Rules

- Scheduled notifications shall respect user quiet hours, locale time zone, and relevant preference settings.
- Time-based or reminder-based notifications shall not be sent outside approved scheduling windows unless business policy requires it.
- The system shall provide an auditable status for scheduled and deferred notifications.
- A user shall be able to manage notification timing and quiet-hour preferences without affecting the integrity of account operations.

### 11.4 Validation

- Time zone, locale, and scheduling windows shall be validated against the user’s active preferences.
- Scheduled follow-up messages shall be checked for staleness before sending.
- Business and operational windows must not conflict with quiet hours or regulatory constraints.

### 11.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Set quiet hours and schedule-related preferences for their account. |
| Artist | Manage schedule-related notification timing for their own account where supported. |
| Admin | Configure system scheduling rules, defaults, and campaign timing policy. |

### 11.6 Edge Cases

- If a scheduled notification becomes obsolete before send time, the system shall cancel or suppress it.
- If user timezone changes, the system shall recalculate the next send window where policy permits.
- If a reminder is delayed due to server outage or temporary unavailability, the system shall keep the schedule status visible without claiming a successful send.

### 11.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Notifications are sent or deferred according to the approved schedule and user preferences.
- Quiet hours are respected across channels where the policy requires it.
- Deferred or scheduled messages remain traceable and not silently lost.

## 12. Retry Strategy

### 12.1 Purpose

The retry strategy ensures that failed notification deliveries are retried in a controlled, policy-aware way without causing user frustration, duplicate delivery, or system overload.

### 12.2 Workflow

1. A notification send attempt fails or is rejected by the relevant channel.
2. The system evaluates retry eligibility based on failure type, channel, recipient state, and business urgency.
3. The system schedules a retry or escalates the failure to support or operations when needed.
4. The system records the final result and any fallback behavior.

### 12.3 Business Rules

- Retry strategy shall avoid infinite loops and prevent duplicate sends when a message is already in a confirmed state.
- Critical notifications may be retried or escalated more aggressively than low-priority ones.
- Retried or deferred notifications shall remain consistent with user preferences and quiet-hours rules.
- A failed delivery shall not be treated as a successful notification in the user interface or admin reporting.

### 12.4 Validation

- Retry counts, backoff intervals, and final failure handling must be validated against policy and channel constraints.
- Duplicate detection shall be enforced before any resend.
- Retry status must be visible to support or admins without exposing user-sensitive details to unauthorized roles.

### 12.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Admin | Define retry policies and review failure states. |
| Support Agent | Review operational failed-delivery cases in support scope. |

### 12.6 Edge Cases

- If a retry becomes stale or the user changes preferences, the system shall stop or suppress the retry.
- If a channel fails while a fallback channel is enabled, the system shall use the approved fallback sequence without violating user preferences.
- If a provider repeatedly rejects a notification, the system shall stop retrying after the maximum policy threshold and create an operational note.

### 12.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Failed notifications are retried according to policy and do not create duplicate delivery loops.
- Support or admins can inspect failed delivery state without unnecessary exposure.
- Both retry and final failure outcomes remain transparent and auditable.

## 13. Preferences

### 13.1 Purpose

Notification preferences let users control which types of updates they receive, which channels they prefer, and when they want notifications to appear. This protects trust, reduces noise, and supports user agency.

### 13.2 Workflow

1. User opens the notification preferences area in the account settings or profile settings.
2. User reviews categories such as orders, messages, sales, promotions, profile updates, security, and more.
3. User selects the preferred channel(s), delivery timing, and categories for each notification area.
4. System validates the settings and stores the updated preference state.
5. Future notifications are created in accordance with the stored preferences.

### 13.3 Business Rules

- Users shall be able to opt in or out of notification categories individually within documented policy rules.
- Security and account-critical notifications may be exempt from full opt-out if required by safety or legal policy.
- Preference changes shall apply to future events without altering active system states except where the policy allows a safe immediate effect.
- Notification preferences shall be available in both Arabic and English, with accessible controls and clear descriptions.

### 13.4 Validation

- Preference values shall be validated for supported categories and channels before being saved.
- Preference changes shall not create inconsistent states between channels or account roles.
- The system shall keep the user’s current preference state authoritative for future notifications.

### 13.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Manage their own notification preferences. |
| Artist | Manage their own notification preferences for marketplace and account activity. |
| Admin | Configure default preference sets and operational templates that shape user options. |

### 13.6 Edge Cases

- If a user disables a category after a trigger is already queued, the system shall suppress or defer the message based on policy.
- If the user chooses a preferred channel that is unavailable or unsupported, the system shall fall back to a permitted alternative or show the user-safe state.
- If a security-critical event is tied to a preference that the user disabled, the system shall still send the minimum required notification where policy requires it.

### 13.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Users can manage notification categories and channels from a clear, accessible settings view.
- Preferences govern future notification eligibility and delivery.
- Security or compliance-critical notifications remain within the approved exception policy.

## 14. Notification Center

### 14.1 Purpose

The Notification Center is the user-facing archive and entry point for in-app notifications. It provides centralized access to all notifications, read states, and actions to review or dismiss items.

### 14.2 Workflow

1. User opens the notification center from the account, app menu, or dashboard area.
2. System displays a list of current and past notifications grouped by category or time where appropriate.
3. User can filter, read, clear, or act on notifications based on the item and documented controls.
4. System records the read or dismissal state and updates the notification count and unread indicator.

### 14.3 Business Rules

- The notification center shall be accessible from the supported web and mobile app surfaces.
- In-app notifications and other user-visible delivery state shall remain consistent with the user’s preference controls and platform policy.
- Unread counts shall reflect current, authoritative state and not be artificially inflated by stale cache or duplicate entries.
- The user shall be able to distinguish between read, unread, and dismissed items without ambiguity.

### 14.4 Validation

- Unread and read states must be revalidated against the notification archive and current user context.
- Filter, search, and grouping logic shall be checked to prevent removed or hidden notifications from appearing.
- Actions such as mark as read, dismiss, or archive must be applied only to authorized notification records.

### 14.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View and manage their own notification center entries. |
| Artist | View and manage their own notification center entries. |
| Admin | View staff operational notifications where policy allows. |

### 14.6 Edge Cases

- If a notification is removed or expired, the system shall hide it without leaving a stale unread indicator.
- If a user opens a notification via deep link or external message, the notification center must still show the correct state after the action.
- If a notification has multiple related actions, the system shall ensure the action opens the right object or workflow without exposing hidden data.

### 14.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Users can review and act on their notifications from a centralized notification center.
- Notification counts and read states remain accurate across sessions.
- Hidden or expired notifications are not presented as active queue items.

## 15. Quiet Hours

### 15.1 Purpose

Quiet hours reduce notification disruption by suppressing non-urgent communications during selected time windows. They help maintain a respectful, low-noise user experience without blocking critical account operations.

### 15.2 Workflow

1. The user sets quiet-hour preferences in the account settings.
2. The system stores the schedule and the relevant restriction type.
3. When a notification is triggered, the scheduling logic evaluates whether the scheduled send falls within the quiet-hours window.
4. The system either postpones the notification, suppresses it, or sends it if the event is an exempt high-priority or security-critical exception.

### 15.3 Business Rules

- Quiet hours shall be configurable by each user and respect local time preferences where available.
- Non-critical notifications shall be deferred or suppressed during quiet hours unless the user or policy allows them.
- Security, fraud, or account-related exceptions shall remain subject to the documented safety exception policy.
- Quiet hours shall not hide a user’s ability to view notifications in the notification center after the quiet window ends.

### 15.4 Validation

- Quiet-hour window calculations shall be validated against time zone and locale settings.
- Exception handling must be consistent with the business rules for critical or operational notifications.
- The system shall not silently bypass quiet hours for unsupported user categories.

### 15.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Configure quiet hours and manage exceptions where supported. |
| Artist | Configure quiet hours and manage exceptions where supported. |
| Admin | Define default policy and monitor the effect of quiet-hours logic. |

### 15.6 Edge Cases

- If a user changes quiet hours while a notification is already queued, the system shall recalculate or suppress the message appropriately.
- If a critical event occurs during quiet hours and the policy allows delivery, the system shall still show the message in the least disruptive channel.
- If the quiet-hours setting overlaps with a global operational message, the system shall preserve the documented exception policy rather than making a silent override.

### 15.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Quiet hours reduce notification disruption without preventing critical account safety notifications when policy requires them.
- Users can manage quiet hours in their account settings.
- Deferred notifications are sent or suppressed according to the stored schedule and policy.

## 16. Localization

### 16.1 Purpose

Localization ensures that notifications are clear, culturally appropriate, and usable in the supported user languages without degrading meaning or accessibility. FANNAN supports Arabic and English as first-class languages.

### 16.2 Workflow

1. A trigger creates a notification event with the required localized content metadata.
2. The system resolves the preferred locale based on user settings, fallback behavior, or service defaults.
3. The system selects the correct template language and renders localized copy and formatting.
4. The notification is sent through the relevant channel with the current locale and any RTL/LTR-safe formatting.

### 16.3 Business Rules

- Email, push, and in-app notification copy shall support Arabic and English in the approved launch languages.
- Notifications must respect RTL/LTR layout and content direction for the selected locale.
- Localized text must remain accurate, concise, and consistent with the underlying event and available template metadata.
- The system shall support approved fallback behavior when a user locale or template translation is unavailable.

### 16.4 Validation

- Locale selection shall be validated before message render and send.
- Template variables and dynamic values shall be checked for correct rendering in both Arabic and English.
- Long-form or subject lines shall remain readable in both script directions.

### 16.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | Receive notifications in their chosen or default supported locale. |
| Artist | Receive notifications in their chosen or default supported locale. |
| Admin | Manage localization defaults and approval for templates. |

### 16.6 Edge Cases

- If a user selects a locale without a complete translation set, the system shall fallback to the approved default language instead of showing incomplete or broken text.
- If a locale or region changes after a scheduled notification is created, the system shall recalculate the correct locale handling before sending.
- If a message has bilingual or mixed content, the system shall preserve the correct directionality and formatting for the selected language.

### 16.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Users receive notification copy in the correct supported locale with clear and usable formatting.
- FANNAN supports Arabic and English notification experience in a consistent and accessible way.
- Fallback and missing-translation handling is transparent and safe.

## 17. Accessibility

### 17.1 Purpose

The notification system must be accessible to all users, including users with vision, motor, and cognitive accessibility needs. This includes readable copy, semantic labeling, screen-reader compatibility, configurable presentation, and clear action affordances.

### 17.2 Workflow

1. A notification is created for a supported user and channel.
2. The system ensures the delivery format includes accessible text, semantic labeling, and visual hierarchy appropriate to the channel.
3. User actions or reads are accessible via keyboard, assistive technology, or device-specific accessibility flows.
4. Users can manage notification preferences and alert states through accessible interactions.

### 17.3 Business Rules

- All notification UI surfaces shall meet accessibility requirements appropriate to the channel and environment.
- Push and email content shall be readable and understandable without relying on color alone.
- Notification centers and action states shall include clear labels, focus states, and semantic structure.
- Important alerts shall remain understandable when assistive technology or low-vision conditions are present.

### 17.4 Validation

- Notification screens and actions shall be reviewed against WCAG 2.2 AA and platform accessibility standards.
- Keyboard access, focus order, and screen-reader labeling shall be validated throughout the notification journey.
- Content readability and contrast shall be checked across both Arabic and English messaging.

### 17.5 Permissions

| Actor | Permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Customer | View and manage notifications via accessible experience and settings. |
| Artist | Receive and manage notifications through accessible experiences. |
| Admin | Configure accessible templates and operational alert presentation. |

### 17.6 Edge Cases

- If a user has reduced motion or screen-reader preferences, the system shall respect them in the notification surface and avoid unnecessary interruptions.
- If a notification contains a deep link or action, the system shall provide a clear label accessible to assistive technology.
- If notification content is truncated for length constraints, the system shall preserve meaning and accessible clarity rather than sacrificing comprehension.

### 17.7 Acceptance Criteria

- Notification center, banners, and action states are accessible to keyboard and assistive-tech users.
- Notification text remains understandable and readable in both supported languages.
- Users can manage and review notifications without relying on visual-only cues.

## 18. Summary

FANNAN’s notifications system is designed to deliver timely, relevant, and respectful updates across customer, artist, and staff journeys. It balances urgency with user agency through clear channel selection, preference control, quiet-hours management, localization, and accessibility. These product requirements establish the notification experience baseline while leaving detailed transport, payload, provider, and queue implementation to the corresponding engineering and infrastructure specifications.
